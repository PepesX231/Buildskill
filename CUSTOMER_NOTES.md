# Customer and comment analysis

Evidence: the owner's supplied course brief and screenshot comments, not new customer interviews. The remarks say the site is beautiful but the learning route is unclear and desktop responsiveness breaks. These indicate comprehension and usability problems; they do not establish willingness to pay or conversion performance.

Primary learners: high school students interested in computing, especially M.5 starting their own body of work; M.6 must check the 12-month course against portfolio deadlines. Entry situations are no project, an existing project they distrust, and self study without direction. Parents are another decision maker who need the duration, price, practical conditions and credible evidence.

Their concrete goals are a game/app/IoT project they can demonstrate and explain, useful skills, a role in a team, opportunities to try competitions and pitch, and a portfolio based on real work. A plausible emotional goal from the owner's brief is confidence and a sense of direction through work they can call their own. Treat this as a messaging hypothesis, not a universal psychological fact about all students.

Previous page issues: OOP/Shader diagrams introduce unfamiliar terms before their purpose; a small timeline conceals how much happens in nine months; small text and isolated image blocks undersell the work; price without nearby deliverables leaves the buyer to reconstruct the value; centered single-card swapping does not show horizontal navigation. No comment proves that more animation will improve sales.

This revision: three large connected highlight panels with real media and visible next-card edge; phase cards show the work, skills and output without opening a dialog; optional dialogs retain depth; price block repeats deliverables next to an approachable project discussion action; footer spans the full background. Real community examples are labeled as examples, not guaranteed student outcomes. No invented awards, faculty, private portfolio, payment policy or schedule.

Next validation with actual target learners: ask what they think they will make, how the three phases differ, what the 19,990 covers, and whether the dates fit their goal. Ask them to find the next slide and the phase detail. Observe hesitation instead of asking only whether it looks nice. No usability study or conversion lift has been claimed.
